## 1. What is Information Security (InfoSec)?
Information Security (InfoSec) protects information and systems against unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, destruction, or disruption.

Its primary objective is to preserve the CIA Triad:
- Confidentiality: Only authorized people can access information.
- Integrity: Information remains accurate and is changed only through authorized actions.
- Availability: Systems and information remain accessible when needed.

Digital transformation increases the amount of valuable data and services online, expanding the potential attack surface and the need for security.
## 2. Basic Digital Infrastructure

| Component | Purpose                                                                                                 |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Client    | A device, such as a PC, laptop, or smartphone, that accesses resources and services.                    |
| Internet  | The global network of interconnected networks that enables communication and access to online services. |
| Server    | A system that provides services or resources to other systems, such as hosting a website.               |
| Network   | Connected devices and systems that communicate with one another.                                        |
| Cloud     | Computing resources delivered over a network, typically using provider-managed data centers.            |
### Cybersecurity teams
- Blue Team: Defends systems, monitors threats, detects attacks, and responds to incidents.
- Red Team: Simulates adversary behavior to test an organization's defenses.
- Purple Team: Facilitates collaboration between Red and Blue Teams to improve detection, response, and overall security.

Penetration testers identify and validate security weaknesses through authorized testing. Their findings help organizations fix vulnerabilities before real attackers exploit them.
## 3. Areas of Information Security
InfoSec covers multiple specialized domains:
1. Network Security — Protecting networks and network communications.
2. Application Security — Protecting software and applications from vulnerabilities.
3. Operational Security (OpSec) — Managing risks associated with operational processes, access, and information handling.
4. Disaster Recovery (DR) and Business Continuity (BC) — Recovering IT systems and maintaining essential business operations during disruptions.
5. Cloud Security — Protecting cloud infrastructure, services, identities, and data.
6. Physical Security — Protecting facilities, equipment, and physical access.
7. Mobile Security — Securing mobile devices, applications, and their data.
8. IoT Security — Protecting internet-connected devices and their communications.

InfoSec also addresses threats such as DDoS attacks, ransomware, advanced persistent threats (APTs), and insider threats.
## 4. Security Concepts: Risk, Threat, and Vulnerability
These three concepts are closely related but have different meanings.
### Threat
Something capable of causing harm to a system or organization.
Example: A cybercriminal attempting to compromise a server.
### Vulnerability
A weakness that a threat could exploit.
Example: An unpatched software flaw or a weak password.
### Risk
The potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability, considering the likelihood and impact of the resulting event.
Example: A server compromise could expose customer data and cause financial or reputational damage.

Relationship: A threat can exploit a vulnerability, creating a risk of harm. Risk management involves assessing these possibilities and implementing appropriate controls to reduce risk.
## 5. Roles in Information Security

| Role                                      | Primary responsibility                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CISO (Chief Information Security Officer) | Oversees the organization's security program, strategy, and risk management.                      |
| Security Architect                        | Designs secure systems, networks, and technical solutions.                                        |
| Penetration Tester                        | Conducts authorized simulated attacks to identify and validate vulnerabilities.                   |
| Incident Response Specialist              | Investigates and responds to security incidents, containing threats and supporting recovery.      |
| Security Analyst                          | Monitors security data, investigates suspicious activity, and identifies potential threats.       |
| Compliance Specialist                     | Helps ensure the organization meets applicable security standards, regulations, and requirements. |
These roles collaborate. For example, penetration testers report weaknesses to system owners and security teams, while analysts and incident responders can use the findings to improve detection and response. Penetration testing reports may also provide evidence for compliance assessments.
## Key Takeaways
- InfoSec protects information and systems, primarily through confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
- Security encompasses technical systems, applications, people, processes, cloud environments, and physical assets.
- Threat = potential cause of harm; vulnerability = exploitable weakness; risk = potential loss or damage.
- Blue Teams defend, Red Teams simulate adversaries, and Purple Teams improve security through collaboration.
- Penetration testing is one part of a broader security program, not a replacement for defensive monitoring, incident response, or risk management.

Remember: Understand the infrastructure, identify weaknesses, evaluate the risk, and help the organization improve its defenses.
