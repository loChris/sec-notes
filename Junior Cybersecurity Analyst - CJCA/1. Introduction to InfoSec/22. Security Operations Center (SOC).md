## What is a SOC?
A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a centralized unit responsible for continuously monitoring, detecting, analyzing, and responding to cybersecurity threats.
## Key Roles
- Tier 1 Analysts: Review alerts, perform initial triage, and conduct basic threat analysis.
- Tier 2 Analysts: Investigate complex incidents and perform deeper analysis.
- Tier 3 Analysts: Handle critical security issues and advanced threat analysis, often as incident responders.
- SOC Manager: Oversees daily operations, coordinates the team, and ensures procedures are followed.
- Threat Hunters: Proactively search for threats that existing security tools may have missed.
- Security Engineers and Architects: Maintain and improve the SOC's tools and infrastructure.
## Purpose and Objectives
- Continuous Monitoring: Watch systems and networks for suspicious activity.
- Threat Detection: Identify potential attacks as early as possible.
- Incident Response: Investigate and contain threats to minimize damage.
- Threat Hunting: Search proactively for hidden threats.
- Business Continuity: Reduce disruptions caused by security incidents.
- Security Improvement: Provide insights that help strengthen the organization's defenses.
## Collaboration
The SOC typically falls under the responsibility of senior security leadership, such as the CISO, while the SOC Manager handles day-to-day operations.

SOC teams work closely with IT departments to fix vulnerabilities and secure systems, and with executives to communicate security risks and incidents.
## Key Takeaway
A SOC is the organization's central hub for cybersecurity monitoring and response. Its team works continuously to detect threats, investigate suspicious activity, contain incidents, and improve overall security.
