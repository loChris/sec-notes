### 1. Definition
Operational Security (OpSec) is the process of identifying critical information and assets, analyzing threats, identifying vulnerabilities, and implementing protective measures to prevent sensitive information from being exposed or compromised.
### 2. OpSec Process
1. Asset Identification: Identify and inventory critical information assets, including data, hardware, and software. Determine their location, importance, and protection requirements.
2. Threat Identification: Identify potential threats that could compromise assets or operations.
3. Vulnerability Identification: Identify weaknesses in systems, configurations, processes, or security controls that threats could exploit.
4. Access Control: Restrict access to information and systems based on user identity, role, and permissions.
5. Monitoring: Continuously monitor systems, access, and security controls, adapting protections as threats and operational conditions change.
### 3. Key Components of OpSec
- Access Control: Use authentication (e.g., Multi-Factor Authentication or MFA) to verify identity and authorization to limit access to required resources. Regularly audit permissions and revoke unnecessary access.
- Asset Management: Maintain an up-to-date inventory of hardware, software, and data to prioritize protection and remediation.
- Change Management: Control system and process changes through proper approval, testing, and documentation to prevent new vulnerabilities.
- Security Awareness Training: Educate employees about phishing, strong passwords, and the proper handling of sensitive information.
### 4. OpSec Responsibilities
- Information Security Team: Coordinates and implements OpSec policies and security measures.
- CISO (Chief Information Security Officer): Oversees security strategy and ensures alignment with business objectives and regulatory requirements.
- IT, HR, and Legal: Collaborate on technical controls, employee-related security procedures, and legal or regulatory compliance.
- All Employees and Executives: Share responsibility for following security policies and protecting organizational assets.
### 5. OpSec Testing
Internal security teams or external consultants assess the effectiveness of operational security controls.
Common testing activities include:
- Penetration Testing: Attempts to identify and exploit security weaknesses.
- Access Control Testing: Checks whether unauthorized access can be obtained.
- Configuration Testing: Identifies insecure system configurations.
- Social Engineering Testing: Evaluates employee resistance to manipulation techniques.
Purpose: Identify weaknesses before malicious actors can exploit them and improve the organization's overall security posture.
### Key Takeaways
- OpSec is a continuous process, not a one-time security assessment.
- Asset identification helps determine what needs protection.
- Threat and vulnerability analysis helps determine what could go wrong and where weaknesses exist.
- Access control, asset management, change management, and employee training are essential OpSec components.
- OpSec is a shared organizational responsibility, requiring cooperation across departments.

Remember: OpSec focuses on protecting critical information and operations by identifying what matters, understanding risks, addressing weaknesses, and continuously evaluating security controls.
