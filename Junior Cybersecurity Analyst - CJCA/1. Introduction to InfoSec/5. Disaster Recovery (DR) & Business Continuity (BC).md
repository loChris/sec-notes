### 1. Disaster Recovery (DR)
Definition: Disaster Recovery focuses on restoring critical IT systems, infrastructure, and data following a disruptive event.

- Goal: Minimize downtime and data loss and restore essential IT services as quickly as possible.
- Common causes: Natural disasters, fires, cyberattacks, and major system failures.
- Key strategies:
    - Data backups and restoration.
    - System and data replication.
    - Failover to alternate sites or cloud environments.
    - Recovery procedures for critical IT infrastructure.
### 2. Business Continuity (BC)
Definition: Business Continuity focuses on maintaining essential business operations during and after a disruption.

- Goal: Keep critical business functions operating, even when normal operations are interrupted.
- Key strategies:
    - Remote work arrangements.
    - Alternative suppliers and resources.
    - Temporary work locations.
    - Alternative processes for delivering essential services.
### 3. DR vs. BC

| Aspect       | Disaster Recovery (DR)                        | Business Continuity (BC)                                |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Focus        | IT systems, infrastructure, and data recovery | Overall business operations                             |
| Objective    | Restore systems and services                  | Maintain essential business functions                   |
| Approach     | Backups, replication, failover                | Alternative workflows, locations, and resources         |
| Scope        | More IT-focused                               | Broader, organization-wide                              |
| Relationship | Supports BC by restoring IT capabilities      | Includes plans to sustain operations during disruptions |
### 4. Recovery Objectives
- RTO (Recovery Time Objective): The maximum targeted time to restore a system or service after a disruption.
Example: Restore a critical application within 4 hours.

- RPO (Recovery Point Objective): The maximum targeted amount of data loss measured in time, determining how far back data recovery can go.
Example: An RPO of 30 minutes means recovery should restore data to a point no more than 30 minutes before the disruption.

Remember: RTO = How quickly must we recover? RPO = How much data can we afford to lose?
### 5. Responsibilities
- Business Continuity Manager: Coordinates DR/BC planning and implementation.
- IT Teams: Manage backups, replication, failover, and technical recovery.
- Operations Teams: Identify critical business functions and implement continuity procedures.
- Executive Leadership: Establish priorities, allocate resources, and approve recovery objectives.
- Penetration Testers / Security Professionals: Identify security weaknesses that could disrupt recovery and assess relevant security controls.
### 6. Testing and Maintenance
DR/BC plans must be tested regularly to verify their effectiveness and ensure staff understand their responsibilities.

Common testing methods:
- Tabletop Exercises: Teams discuss how they would respond to a simulated disaster.
- Simulations: Participants practice procedures in a controlled scenario.
- Failover Testing: Systems are switched to backup infrastructure to verify recovery capabilities.
- Full-Scale Exercises: Organizations test broader recovery procedures, potentially involving alternate sites and multiple departments.

Testing frequency depends on organizational needs, complexity, and regulatory requirements. Annual testing is common, though some systems and procedures may require more frequent testing.
### 7. Why DR/BC Matters
- Reduces downtime and financial losses.
- Protects data and critical services.
- Maintains customer trust and organizational reputation.
- Helps satisfy regulatory requirements.
- Improves preparedness for unexpected disruptions.

Key takeaway: DR restores IT systems and data; BC keeps the business operating. Both require defined recovery objectives, documented procedures, regular testing, and coordination across the organization.
