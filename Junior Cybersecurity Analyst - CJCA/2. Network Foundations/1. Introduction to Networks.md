## What is a Network?

A network is a collection of interconnected devices that communicate by sending and receiving data and sharing resources.

- Nodes: Devices connected to a network, such as computers, smartphones, printers, and servers.
- Links: Wired or wireless pathways that connect devices.
- Data Sharing: The exchange of information between connected devices.

## Why Are Networks Important?

- Resource Sharing: Share hardware, software, and other resources.
- Communication: Enable email, messaging, and video calls.
- Data Access: Access files and databases across connected devices.
- Collaboration: Allow people to work together from different locations.

## Types of Networks

### LAN — Local Area Network

Connects devices within a small geographical area, such as a home, school, or office.

- Scope: Small, localized area.
- Ownership: Usually managed by one person or organization.
- Speed: Generally high data transfer rates.
- Media: Ethernet cables or Wi-Fi.
- Example: A home network connecting laptops, phones, and smart TVs.

### WAN — Wide Area Network

Connects networks across large geographical areas, such as cities, countries, or continents.

- Scope: Large geographical area.
- Ownership: Often involves multiple organizations and service providers.
- Speed: Generally higher latency than a LAN because of longer distances and infrastructure.
- Media: Fiber optics, satellite links, and leased telecommunications lines.
- Example: The Internet, which connects networks around the world.

## LAN vs. WAN

|Feature|LAN|WAN|
|---|---|---|
|Coverage|Small area|Large area|
|Ownership|Usually one organization|Often multiple providers|
|Performance|Generally faster, lower latency|Generally higher latency|
|Maintenance|Simpler, less expensive|More complex, more expensive|
|Example|Home or office network|Internet|

## How LANs and WANs Work Together

A LAN allows devices to communicate locally, while a WAN connects networks over greater distances.

For example, when you access a website from home:

1. Your device connects to your home router, joining the LAN.
2. Your router forwards traffic toward your Internet Service Provider (ISP).
3. Your ISP provides connectivity to the wider Internet.
4. Your traffic travels across interconnected networks to reach the website.

Key Terms:

- Router: Connects networks and forwards data between them.
- Modem: Converts signals to enable communication over the access technology used by an ISP.
- ISP (Internet Service Provider): A company that provides Internet access.

Businesses also use WANs to connect LANs in different offices, allowing employees to share information and access centralized resources.

## Key Takeaway

A LAN connects devices locally, while a WAN connects networks across larger geographical areas. Together, they enable communication and resource sharing from a home network to the global Internet.
