## What is a Wireless Network?

A wireless network uses radio waves or other wireless signals to connect devices and exchange data without physical cables.

Advantages

- Mobility: Devices can move within the coverage area.
- Easy Installation: Requires less physical cabling.
- Scalability: Adding devices is relatively simple.

Disadvantages

- Interference: Walls and other electronics can disrupt signals.
- Security Risks: Transmissions may be intercepted without proper security.
- Speed Limitations: Wireless connections may be slower than comparable wired connections.

## Wireless Router

A wireless router combines two main functions:

- Routing: Directs data packets to their destinations.
- Wireless Access Point: Provides Wi-Fi connectivity.

Main components:

- WAN port: Connects to the internet source, such as a modem.
- LAN ports: Connect wired devices to the local network.
- Antennas: Transmit and receive wireless signals.
- Processor and memory: Handle routing and network management.

## Mobile Hotspot

A mobile hotspot allows a smartphone or another hotspot device to share its cellular internet connection over Wi-Fi.

- Uses cellular networks such as 4G or 5G.
- Allows laptops and tablets to connect to the internet.
- Has a limited wireless range and can drain the host device's battery.
- Is typically protected by a password.

## Cell Towers

Cell towers provide cellular coverage by transmitting and receiving radio signals between mobile devices and the cellular network.

- Macro cells: Provide broad coverage over several kilometers, often in rural areas.
- Micro/small cells: Provide coverage in densely populated areas and fill gaps in larger cells.

As users move, their devices switch between cell towers to maintain connectivity. The towers connect to the core network through backhaul links, such as fiber-optic or microwave links.

## Frequencies in Wireless Communications

Wireless networks use different radio frequencies, measured in hertz (Hz).

| Frequency      | Characteristics                                                                               |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi  | Better wall penetration and range, but more susceptible to interference.                      |
| 5 GHz Wi-Fi    | Generally faster, but has a shorter range.                                                    |
| Cellular bands | Used by 4G and 5G, ranging from lower frequencies to higher frequencies for some 5G services. |

In general:

- Lower frequencies: Travel farther but typically carry less data.
- Higher frequencies: Can carry more data but generally have shorter ranges.
- Interference and congestion: Occur when multiple devices use the same frequencies.

Government agencies, such as the FCC in the United States, regulate frequency allocations to help prevent interference.

## Key Takeaway

Wireless communication relies on different technologies for different purposes:

- Wi-Fi: Connects devices to a local wireless network.
- Cellular networks: Provide wide-area connectivity through cell towers.
- Mobile hotspots: Share a cellular connection with other devices.

Understanding their range, speed, frequencies, and security risks helps explain how wireless devices communicate.
